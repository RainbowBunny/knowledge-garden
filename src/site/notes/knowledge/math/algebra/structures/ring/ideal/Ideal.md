## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $(R, +, \cdot)$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/Ring\|Ring]].
> - $I \subseteq R$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Subgroup\|Subgroup]] of $(R, +)$.

> [!definition] Ideal
> Extends:: [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Subgroup\|Subgroup]].
> Requires:: [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/Ring\|Ring]].
>
> ---
> A [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Subgroup\|Subgroup]] $I$ of $(R, +)$ is
> - a **left ideal** of $R$ if $rI \subseteq I$ for all $r \in R$:
> $$(\forall r \in R)(\forall a \in I): ra \in I$$
> - a **right ideal** of $R$ if $Ir \subseteq I$ for all $r \in R$:
> $$(\forall r \in R)(\forall a \in I): ar \in I$$
> - a **two-sided ideal** if both.
>
> `[Alu III.3.1]` `[Jud Rem 16.28]`

### Principal Ideal

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $a \in R$: an element.

> [!definition] Ideal generated by an element
> Let $a \in R$ be any element of [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/Ring\|Ring]] $(R, +, \cdot)$. Then:
> - $Ra = \{ra \mid r \in R\}$ is a left ideal of $R$ (the left ideal generated by $a$).
> - $aR = \{ar \mid r \in R\}$ is a right ideal of $R$ (the right ideal generated by $a$).
>
> `[Alu III.4.1]`

> [!definition] Principal Ideal
> If $R$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/special ring/Commutative Ring\|Commutative Ring]], then $aR = Ra$ and this subset is the **principal ideal generated by** $a$, written $(a)$ (Judson: $\langle a \rangle$). In particular $(1) = R$.
>
> `[Alu III.4.1]` `[Jud §16.3]`

### Finitely Generated Ideal

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $E \subseteq R$: a subset.
> - $a_1, \dots, a_n \in R$: elements.

> [!definition] Ideal Generated by a Set
> $\langle E \rangle$ is the (left, right or two-sided) ideal **generated by** $E$: the smallest such ideal containing $E$. For finitely many elements, $\langle a_1, \dots, a_n \rangle = \langle \{a_1, \dots, a_n\} \rangle$. `[Pla25 Not 1.2]`

> [!definition] Finitely Generated Ideal
> An ideal $I$ (left, right or two-sided) of $R$ is **finitely generated**, or **of finite type**, if there are $a_1, \dots, a_n \in R$ with
> $$I = \langle a_1, \dots, a_n \rangle.$$
> `[Pla25 Def 1.1]`. A [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/ideal/Ideal#Principal Ideal\|principal ideal]] is the case $n = 1$.

## Variant

> [!remark] Ideals as subrings
> In `[Jud §16.3]` a ring need not have an identity, and an ideal is defined as a *subring* $I$ with $ar, ra \in I$ for $a \in I$, $r \in R$. Here rings have $1$ and a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/ring/subring/Subring\|Subring]] must contain $1$, so the only ideal that is a subring is $R$ itself; that is why the definition above asks only for a subgroup of $(R, +)$.

## Example

> [!example] Ideals of $\mathbb Z$
> Every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is principal: it is $n\mathbb Z = (n)$ for some $n \geq 0$. `[Jud Thm 16.25]`
