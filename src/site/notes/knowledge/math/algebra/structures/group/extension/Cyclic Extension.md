## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $G$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/Group\|Group]].
> - $N \trianglelefteq G$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Normal Subgroup\|Normal Subgroup]].
> - $n \geq 1$: an integer.

> [!definition] Cyclic Extension
> Extends:: [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/extension/Group Extension\|Group Extension]].
>
> ---
> $G$ is a **cyclic extension** of $N$ (of degree $n$) if $G/N$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/special group/Cyclic Group\|Cyclic Group]] of order $n$:
> $$1 \to N \to G \to C_n \to 1.$$
>
> `[AGW16 §1.1]`

### Extension type

> [!definition] Extension Type
> An **extension type** for a group $N$ is a quadruple $(N, n, \tau, v)$ with $n \geq 1$, $v \in N$ and $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(N)$ such that
> $$\tau^n = \gamma_v,$$
> $$\tau(v) = v,$$
> where $\gamma_v(x) = vxv^{-1}$ is the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/homomorphism/Inner Automorphism\|Inner Automorphism]] given by $v$.
>
> `[AGW16 Def 1]` states only $\tau^n = \gamma_v$; `[AGW16 §1.1]` derives $\tau(v) = v$ for every type that comes from a group. The second condition is needed for the construction below — see the remark.

> [!remark] The type of a cyclic extension `[AGW16 §1.1]`
> Let $G/N = \langle aN \rangle$ have order $n$. Put $\tau(x) = axa^{-1}$ for $x \in N$, and $v = a^n \in N$. Then
> - $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(N)$, because $N$ is normal;
> - $\tau(v) = a\, a^n a^{-1} = v$;
> - $\tau^n(x) = a^n x a^{-n} = vxv^{-1}$.
>
> So $(N, n, \tau, v)$ is an extension type, and $G = \langle N, a \mid axa^{-1} = \tau(x)\ (x \in N),\ a^n = v \rangle$.

## Property

> [!proposition] Normal form — every element is $x a^k$ with $0 \leq k < n$, uniquely
> **Scope**
> - $G/N = \langle aN \rangle$ of order $n$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> Every $g \in G$ is $g = x a^k$ for a unique $x \in N$ and a unique $k \in \{0, \dots, n - 1\}$, and
> $$(x a^i)(y a^j) = \begin{cases} x\, \tau^i(y)\, a^{i+j}, & i + j < n, \\ x\, \tau^i(y)\, v\, a^{i+j-n}, & i + j \geq n. \end{cases}$$
>
> $gN = a^k N$ for one $k$ modulo $n$, since $aN$ has order $n$. The rule is the multiplication `[AGW16]` uses in the proof of Theorem 2.

> [!theorem] Cyclic Extension Theorem — every extension type determines a group
> **Scope**
> - $(N, n, \tau, v)$: an extension type, with $\tau(v) = v$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> The set of pairs $(x, a^i)$, $x \in N$, $0 \leq i < n$, with the multiplication above, is a group $G$. It contains $N$ as the normal subgroup $\{(x, a^0)\}$, with $G/N$ cyclic of order $n$ generated by the class of $a = (e, a)$, and its extension type for $a$ is $(N, n, \tau, v)$.
>
> `[AGW16 Thm 2]`, attributed there to `[Wil05]`. Associativity uses $\tau^n = \gamma_v$, through $v^r \tau^{m - nr}(z) = \tau^m(z) v^r$, and also $\tau(v) = v$.

> [!remark] Why $\tau(v) = v$ cannot be dropped
> Take $N = C_2 \times C_2$ with basis $e_1, e_2$, $n = 2$, $\tau$ the swap of $e_1$ and $e_2$, and $v = e_1$. Then $\tau^2 = \mathrm{id} = \gamma_v$, since $N$ is abelian, but $\tau(v) = e_2 \neq v$. With the rule above,
> $$\big((e, a)(e, a)\big)(e, a) = (e_1, a),$$
> $$(e, a)\big((e, a)(e, a)\big) = (e, a)(e_1, a^0) = (\tau(e_1), a) = (e_2, a).$$
> Associativity fails, so the theorem needs both conditions. `⚠️ CLAUDE – verify:` this computation is mine; I read `[AGW16 Def 1]` and its proof of Theorem 2 only through a summarizing tool, which reported that neither uses $\tau(v) = v$.

### When two types give the same group

> [!definition] Equivalent and Conjugate Types
> - Two extension types are **equivalent** if they determine isomorphic groups `[AGW16 Def 3]`.
> - $(N, n, \tau, v)$ and $(N', n, \sigma, w)$ are **conjugate** if there is an isomorphism $\phi : N \to N'$ with $\sigma = \phi \tau \phi^{-1}$ and $w = \phi(v)$ `[AGW16 Def 4]`.

> [!definition] Norm Map
> For $\tau \in \mathrm{Aut}(N)$ and $n \geq 1$,
> $$\mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x) = x\, \tau(x)\, \tau^2(x) \cdots \tau^{n-1}(x).$$
>
> `[AGW16 Notation 7]`

> [!lemma] Powers of $xa$ — $(xa)^n = \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, a^n$
> **Scope**
> - $N \trianglelefteq G$, $a \in G$ with $\tau(x) = axa^{-1}$ on $N$.
> - $x \in N$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$(xa)^n = \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, a^n.$$
>
> `[AGW16 Lemma 8]`: $(xa)^n = x \cdot axa^{-1} \cdot a^2 x a^{-2} \cdots a^{n-1} x a^{-(n-1)} \cdot a^n$.

> [!proposition] Moves that keep the group — the types equivalent to $(N, n, \tau, v)$
> **Scope**
> - $(N, n, \tau, v)$: an extension type.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> - Conjugate types are equivalent `[AGW16 Lemma 5]`.
> - For $x \in N$: $(N, n, \tau, v)$ is equivalent to $(N, n, \gamma_x \circ \tau, \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, v)$ `[AGW16 Lemma 9]`.
> - For $N$ abelian and $x \in N$: $(N, n, \tau, v)$ is equivalent to $(N, n, \tau, \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, v)$ `[AGW16 Cor 10]`.
> - For $N$ finite abelian and $i$ prime to $|N|$: $(N, n, \tau, v)$ and $(N, n, \tau, v^i)$ are conjugate `[AGW16 Lemma 6]`.
> - For $i$ prime to $n$: $(N, n, \tau, v)$ is equivalent to $(N, n, \tau^i, v^i)$ `[AGW16 Lemma 11]`.

> [!proof] Why the second move — replace the generator $a$ by $b = xa$
> $b$ also generates $G/N$. Conjugation by $b$ is $\gamma_x \circ \tau$, and $b^n = \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, a^n = \mathcal N_{\tau, n}(x)\, v$ by the lemma. So the same group has the second type for the generator $b$. Likewise $a^i$ generates $G/N$ when $i$ is prime to $n$, which gives the last move.

## Example

> [!example] Split types
> $v = e$ gives the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/extension/Semidirect Product\|Semidirect Product]] $N \rtimes_\tau C_n$: the elements $a^k$ form a complement isomorphic to $C_n$.

> [!example] Extensions of $C_8$ by $C_2$
> $N = \langle x \mid x^8 = e \rangle$, $n = 2$. Since $N$ is abelian, $\tau^2 = \mathrm{id}$, so $\tau(x) \in \{x, x^3, x^5, x^7\}$, and $v$ must be fixed by $\tau$. The types $(\tau, v) = (x \mapsto x, x)$, $(x \mapsto x^7, x^4)$ and the four split types give $C_{16}$, the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/example/Generalized Quaternion Group\|Generalized Quaternion Group]] $Q_{16}$, and $C_8 \times C_2$, $D_8$, the semidihedral and the modular group — [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/classification/Groups of Order 16\|Groups of Order 16]].
