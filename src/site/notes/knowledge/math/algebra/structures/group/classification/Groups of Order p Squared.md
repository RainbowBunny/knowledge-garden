## Definition

> [!theorem] Groups of Order p² — abelian, and either $C_{p^2}$ or $C_p \times C_p$
> ### Scope
> - $p$: a [[knowledge/math/number theory/Prime\|Prime]].
> - $G$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/Group\|Group]] of order $p^2$.
>
> ---
> ### Condition
> - None: $p = 2$ is included.
>
> ---
> ### Property
> $G$ is abelian, and
> $$G \cong C_{p^2} \quad \text{or} \quad G \cong C_p \times C_p.$$
> The two are not isomorphic.
>
> `[ConConj Cor 6.11]` for abelian; `[ConCauApp Rem 3.10]` for the two groups.

## Property

> [!proof] Why — the center is everything
> $Z(G)$ is nontrivial, since $G$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/special group/p-Group\|p-Group]] `[ConConj Thm 6.8]`, so $|Z(G)| \in \{p, p^2\}$. Suppose $|Z(G)| = p$ and take $g \notin Z(G)$. If $g$ had order $p^2$, $G = \langle g \rangle$ would be abelian; so $\langle g \rangle$ has order $p$ and meets $Z(G)$ trivially. Then $|Z(G) \langle g \rangle| = p^2$ by the product formula ([[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Normalizer#Products of subgroups\|Normalizer#Products of subgroups]]), so $G = Z(G) \langle g \rangle$ is generated by $g$ and central elements: abelian, against $Z(G) \neq G$. So $G = Z(G)$. An abelian group of order $p^2$ either has an element of order $p^2$, and is cyclic, or has all nonidentity elements of order $p$; then for $a \neq e$ and $b \notin \langle a \rangle$, $G = \langle a \rangle \times \langle b \rangle \cong C_p \times C_p$. The two differ in their largest element order.

## Example

> [!example] Order 4 and 9
> - Order 4: $C_4$ and $C_2 \times C_2$, the Klein four-group.
> - Order 9: $C_9$ and $C_3 \times C_3$.
