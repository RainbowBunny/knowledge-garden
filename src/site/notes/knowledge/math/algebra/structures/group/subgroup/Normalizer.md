## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $G$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/Group\|Group]].
> - $H \leq G$: a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Subgroup\|Subgroup]].

> [!definition] Normalizer
> The **normalizer** of $H$ in $G$ is
> $$N_G(H) = \{g \in G \mid gHg^{-1} = H\}.$$
> It is a subgroup of $G$.
>
> `[Jud §15.1]`, written $N(H)$ there.

## Property

> [!proposition] The largest subgroup in which $H$ is normal
> **Scope**
> - $H \leq G$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> - $H \trianglelefteq N_G(H)$.
> - $H$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Normal Subgroup\|Normal Subgroup]] of $G$ iff $N_G(H) = G$.
>
> Both are the definition read off: $N_G(H)$ is the set of $g$ for which conjugation by $g$ preserves $H$.

> [!proposition] Number of conjugates — $[G : N_G(H)]$
> **Scope**
> - $G$: a finite group, $H \leq G$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> The number of subgroups $gHg^{-1}$, $g \in G$, equals $[G : N_G(H)]$.
>
> $N_G(H)$ is the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/action/Stabilizer\|Stabilizer]] of $H$ for the action of $G$ on its subgroups by conjugation; apply the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/action/Stabilizer#Property\|orbit–stabilizer theorem]]. `[Jud Lemma 15.1.6]` counts the conjugates under a subgroup: $K$ has $[H : N(K) \cap H]$ conjugates $hKh^{-1}$, $h \in H$. With $H = P$ a Sylow subgroup this gives $n_p = [G : N_G(P)]$ — [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Sylow Theorems\|Sylow Theorems]].

### Products of subgroups

> [!proposition] A normalizing subgroup — if $H \leq N_G(K)$, then $HK$ is a subgroup
> **Scope**
> - $H, K \leq G$ with $H \leq N_G(K)$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$HK = KH \leq G.$$
>
> `[Dart13 Problem 2]`. In particular $HK$ is a subgroup when $K$ is normal in $G$ `[ConSD]`; then the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Normal Subgroup#Property\|second isomorphism theorem]] describes $HK/K$.

> [!proposition] Product formula — $|HK| = |H||K| / |H \cap K|$
> **Scope**
> - $H, K \leq G$: finite subgroups, $HK$ not necessarily a subgroup.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$|HK| = \frac{|H| \, |K|}{|H \cap K|}.$$
>
> `[ConSD]`

> [!corollary] Complements by counting — when $|G| = |H||K|$, $G = HK$ iff $H \cap K = \{e\}$
> **Scope**
> - $H \trianglelefteq G$, $K \leq G$, $G$ finite.
> - $|G| = |H| \, |K|$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$G = HK \iff H \cap K = \{e\}.$$
>
> $HK$ is a subgroup, and by the product formula $|HK| = |G| / |H \cap K|$. With these hypotheses $G$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/extension/Semidirect Product\|Semidirect Product]] $H \rtimes K$.

## Example

> [!example] A non-normal subgroup
> In the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/special group/Symmetric Group\|Symmetric Group]] $S_3$, the subgroup $H = \{e, (1\,2)\}$ has $N_{S_3}(H) = H$, so it has $[S_3 : H] = 3$ conjugates: the three subgroups generated by transpositions.
