## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $G$: a finite [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/Group\|Group]].
> - $p$: a [[knowledge/math/number theory/Prime\|Prime]], with $|G| = p^k m$ and $p \nmid m$.

> [!definition] Sylow Subgroup
> A subgroup of $G$ of order $p^k$, the highest power of $p$ dividing $|G|$, is a **Sylow $p$-subgroup** of $G$. The set of them is $\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$, and their number is
> $$n_p = |\mathrm{Syl}_p(G)|.$$
>
> `[ConSyl Def 1.1, Thm 1.9, §2]`

> [!remark] Judson's definition
> `[Jud §15.1]` defines a Sylow $p$-subgroup as a maximal [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/special group/p-Group\|$p$-subgroup]] of $G$. The [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Sylow Theorems\|Sylow Theorems]] show the two definitions agree for finite groups: every $p$-subgroup lies in a subgroup of order $p^k$.

## Property

> [!proposition] Normal iff unique — a Sylow $p$-subgroup is normal iff $n_p = 1$
> **Scope**
> - $P \in \mathrm{Syl}_p(G)$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$P \trianglelefteq G \iff n_p = 1.$$
>
> `[ConSylApp Thm 3.1]`: conjugates of $P$ are Sylow $p$-subgroups, and all Sylow $p$-subgroups are conjugate ([[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Sylow Theorems\|Sylow Theorems]]).

> [!proposition] Counting elements of prime order
> **Scope**
> - $|G| = pm$ with $p \nmid m$, so the Sylow $p$-subgroups have prime order $p$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> Two distinct Sylow $p$-subgroups meet only in $e$, so $G$ has exactly
> $$n_p \, (p - 1)$$
> elements of order $p$.
>
> A subgroup of prime order is generated by any of its nonidentity elements. Conrad uses this count to force a normal Sylow subgroup, for instance in order 30 `[ConSylApp Lemma 5.3, Thm 5.9]` — [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/classification/Groups of Order 30\|Groups of Order 30]].

## Example

> [!example] Sylow subgroups of $S_3$
> $|S_3| = 6 = 2 \cdot 3$. The Sylow 3-subgroup $A_3 = \langle (1\,2\,3) \rangle$ is unique, hence normal; there are three Sylow 2-subgroups, generated by the transpositions ([[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Normalizer#Example\|Normalizer#Example]]).
