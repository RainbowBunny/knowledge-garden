## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $m$: the dimension of the ambient space $\mathbb R^m$.
> - $n \leq m$: the number of generating vectors (the rank of $\Lambda$).
> - $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n \in \mathbb R^m$: linearly independent vectors.
> - $V = [\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n] \in \mathbb R^{m \times n}$: the matrix whose columns are the $\mathbf v_i$.

> [!definition] Lattices
> Requires:: [[knowledge/math/algebra/linear algebra/Vector Spaces\|Vector Spaces]].
>
> ---
> The **lattice** $\Lambda$ generated by $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n$ is the set of their linear combinations with coefficients in $\mathbb Z$:
> $$\Lambda = \{a_1 \mathbf v_1 + a_2 \mathbf v_2 + \cdots + a_n \mathbf v_n : a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb Z\}.$$
> Equivalently, with the matrix $V$,
> $$\Lambda = \{V \mathbf x : \mathbf x \in \mathbb Z^n\}.$$
>
> `[HPS]` `[Reg04 L1 Def 1]` `[Gal12 Def 16.1.1]`

### Fundamental Domain

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $L$: a lattice of dimension $n$.
> - $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n$: a basis of $L$.
> - $t_1, \dots, t_n \in [0, 1)$: real coefficients.

> [!definition] Fundamental Domain
> The **fundamental domain** (or **fundamental parallelepiped**) of $L$ for the basis $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n$ is the set
> $$\mathcal F(\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n) = \{t_1 \mathbf v_1 + t_2 \mathbf v_2 + \cdots + t_n \mathbf v_n : 0 \leq t_i < 1\}.$$
>
> `[HPS]` `[Reg04 L1 Def 3]`

### Volume

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $L$: a lattice of dimension $n$.
> - $\mathcal F$: a fundamental domain for $L$.

> [!definition] Covolume
> The $n$-dimensional volume of $\mathcal F$ is the **determinant** of $L$ (or its **covolume**):
> $$\det(L) = \mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F).$$
> It does not depend on the choice of $\mathcal F$ ([[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/lattice/Lattices#Determinant\|Invariance of the determinant]]).
>
> `[HPS]` `[Reg04 L1 Def 5]` `[Gal12 Def 16.1.8]`

### Successive Minima

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $\Lambda$: a lattice of rank $n$.
> - $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$: an index.

> [!definition] Successive Minima
> The $i$-th **successive minimum** $\lambda_i(\Lambda)$ is the smallest $r$ such that $\Lambda$ has $i$ linearly independent vectors of norm at most $r$.
> - $\lambda_1(\Lambda)$ is the length of a shortest nonzero vector.
> - With the $\ell_\infty$ norm in place of the Euclidean norm one writes $\lambda_i^\infty(\Lambda)$.
>
> `[Pei16 §2.2.1]` `[Reg04 L1 Def 7]`

### Smoothing Parameter

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb Z^n$: an $n$-dimensional lattice.
> - $\Lambda^*$: its dual lattice, see [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/lattice/Lattice Smoothing#Definition\|dual lattice]].
> - $\epsilon > 0$: a real number.
> - $\rho_s(\mathbf x) = \exp(-\pi \|\mathbf x\|^2 / s^2)$: the Gaussian function of width $s > 0$ ([[knowledge/math/set theory/function/Unnormalized Gaussian Function#Definition\|Unnormalized Gaussian Function]]), summed over a set: $\rho_s(A) = \sum_{\mathbf x \in A} \rho_s(\mathbf x)$ `[Reg04 L11]`.

> [!definition] Smoothing Parameter
> The **smoothing parameter** $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ is the smallest real $r > 0$ such that
> $$\rho_{1/r}(\Lambda^* \setminus \{\mathbf 0\}) \leq \epsilon.$$
>
> `[SSEKYZ22 Def 5]` `[GPV08 Def 2.5]`

> [!remark] Any lattice
> [GPV08 Def 2.5] (after Micciancio–Regev) defines $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ the same way for every lattice, not only for $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb Z^n$; the lemmas under [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/lattice/Lattices#Smoothing\|#Smoothing]] hold in that generality.

## Property

### Bases

> [!proposition] Change of basis — two bases of a lattice differ by an integer matrix of determinant $\pm 1$
> **Scope**
> - $L$: a lattice of rank $n$.
> - $B, B'$: two bases of $L$, written as matrices whose columns are the basis vectors.
>
> ---
> **Property** — there is a matrix $U$ with
> - $U \in \mathbb Z^{n \times n}$;
> - $B' = B U$;
> - $\det U = \pm 1$.
>
> `[HPS]` `[Reg04 L1 Lem 3]` `[Gal12 Lem 16.1.6]`

> [!theorem] Lattices are discrete subgroups — a subset of $\mathbb R^m$ is a lattice iff it is a discrete additive subgroup
> **Scope**
> - $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb R^m$: a subset.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> - $\Lambda$ is a lattice $\iff$ $\Lambda$ is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/group/subgroup/Discrete Additive Subgroup\|Discrete Additive Subgroup]] of $\mathbb R^m$.
>
> `[Gal12 Lem 16.1.3]` `[Pei16 §2.2.1]`

### Tiling

> [!proposition] Tiling by translates of $\mathcal F$ — every vector of $\mathbb R^n$ is uniquely a point of $\mathcal F$ plus a lattice vector
> **Scope**
> - $L \subset \mathbb R^n$: a lattice of dimension $n$.
> - $\mathcal F$: a fundamental domain for $L$.
>
> ---
> **Property** — for every $\mathbf w \in \mathbb R^n$ there are a unique $\mathbf t \in \mathcal F$ and a unique $\mathbf v \in L$ with
> $$\mathbf w = \mathbf t + \mathbf v.$$
> Equivalently, the translates $\mathcal F + \mathbf v = \{\mathbf t + \mathbf v : \mathbf t \in \mathcal F\}$, as $\mathbf v$ ranges over $L$, exactly cover $\mathbb R^n$:
> $$\mathbb R^n = \bigsqcup_{\mathbf v \in L} (\mathcal F + \mathbf v).$$
>
> `[HPS]` `[Reg04 L1]` (remark after Def 3)

### Determinant

> [!proposition] Hadamard's Inequality — $\det L$ is at most the product of the lengths of any basis
> **Scope**
> - $L$: a lattice of rank $n$.
> - $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n$: any basis of $L$.
> - $\mathcal F$: a fundamental domain for $L$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\det L = \mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F),$$
> $$\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F) \leq \|\mathbf v_1\| \, \|\mathbf v_2\| \cdots \|\mathbf v_n\|.$$
>
> `[HPS]` `[Gal12 Ex 16.1.15]`

> [!proposition] Volume as a determinant — $\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F) = |\det F|$ for the matrix $F$ of basis rows
> **Scope**
> - $L \subset \mathbb R^n$: a lattice of dimension $n$.
> - $\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n$: a basis of $L$, with coordinates $\mathbf v_i = (r_{i1}, r_{i2}, \dots, r_{in})$.
> - $F = F(\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n)$: the matrix whose rows are the coordinates of the $\mathbf v_i$,
>   $$F = \begin{bmatrix}
>   r_{11} & r_{12} & \cdots & r_{1n} \\
>   r_{21} & r_{22} & \cdots & r_{2n} \\
>   \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
>   r_{n1} & r_{n2} & \cdots & r_{nn}
>   \end{bmatrix}.$$
> - $\mathcal F = \mathcal F(\mathbf v_1, \dots, \mathbf v_n)$: the associated fundamental domain.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F) = |\det F|.$$
>
> `[HPS]` `[Gal12 Def 16.1.8]`

> [!corollary] Invariance of the determinant — every fundamental domain of $L$ has the same volume
> **Scope**
> - $L \subset \mathbb R^n$: a lattice of dimension $n$.
> - $\mathcal F, \mathcal F'$: two fundamental domains for $L$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F) = \mathrm{Vol}(\mathcal F').$$
> Hence $\det(L)$ is an invariant of $L$, independent of the fundamental domain used to compute it.
>
> `[HPS]` `[Gal12 §16.1]`

### Shortest Vectors

> [!remark] Facts about SVP
> See [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/lattice/Lattice Problem\|Lattice Problem]] for the problem.
> 1. SVP is NP-hard under randomized reductions (Ajtai) `[HR12 §1]`. So there is very strong theoretical evidence that SVP is hard in the worst case.
> 2. The fastest algorithm known for solving SVP, heuristic lattice sieving, has running time $2^{0.292n + o(n)}$ `[DGLM24 §1]` (which quotes $2^{0.2925n + o(n)}$).
> 3. The fastest quantum algorithm known for solving SVP, also heuristic, has running time $2^{0.2563n + o(n)}$ `[DGLM24 §1]`.

### Smoothing

> [!lemma] Consequence of Smoothing — above $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ every coset of $\Lambda$ has nearly the same Gaussian mass as $\Lambda$
> **Scope**
> - $\Lambda$: an $n$-dimensional lattice.
> - $\epsilon \in (0, 1)$.
> - $r \geq \eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$: a width at least the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/lattice/Lattices#Smoothing Parameter\|smoothing parameter]].
> - $\mathbf c \in \mathbb R^n$: a shift.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\frac{1 - \epsilon}{1 + \epsilon} \cdot \rho_r(\Lambda) \leq \rho_r(\mathbf c + \Lambda),$$
> $$\rho_r(\mathbf c + \Lambda) \leq \rho_r(\Lambda).$$
>
> `[GPV08 Lem 2.7]` (implicit in Micciancio–Regev, Lemma 4.4); `[SSEKYZ22 Lem 4]` prints the interval with its endpoints swapped.

> [!lemma] Upper-Bound of Smoothing Parameter — $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ is at most $\sqrt{\ln(2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1}))/\pi}$ over $\lambda_1^\infty(\Lambda^*)$
> **Scope**
> - $\Lambda$: an $n$-dimensional lattice, with dual $\Lambda^*$.
> - $\epsilon > 0$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda) \leq \frac{\sqrt{\ln(2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1})) / \pi}}{\lambda_1^{\infty}(\Lambda^*)}.$$
>
> `[SSEKYZ22 Lem 6]` (after Peikert 2008)

> [!lemma] Sandwiches of Smoothing Parameter — $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ lies between $\lambda_n(\Lambda)/\sqrt n$ and $\lambda_n(\Lambda)\sqrt{\ln(2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1}))/\pi}$
> **Scope**
> - $\Lambda$: a lattice of rank $n$.
> - $\epsilon \in (0, 1/2)$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$\lambda_n(\Lambda) / \sqrt{n} \leq \eta_\epsilon(\Lambda),$$
> $$\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda) \leq \lambda_n(\Lambda) \cdot \sqrt{\ln (2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1})) / \pi}.$$
>
> `[SSEKYZ22 Lem 7]`. The upper bound holds for every $\epsilon > 0$: it is Micciancio–Regev, Lemma 3.3, recalled in `[GPV08 §3]`.
> ⚠️ CLAUDE – verify: the lower bound fails for $\Lambda = \mathbb Z$ ($n = 1$, $\lambda_1 = 1$): numerically $\eta_{0.1}(\mathbb Z) \approx 0.977$ and $\eta_{0.49}(\mathbb Z) \approx 0.67$, both $< 1$. Check the hypotheses in the lemma [SSEKYZ22] cites (its ref. [41], Lemma 7). [Reg04 L12 Cor 8] states instead $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda) \geq \lambda_n(\Lambda)/n$ for $\epsilon < 1/100$.

### Transference

> [!lemma] Banaszczyk's transference theorem — $1 \leq \lambda_i(\Lambda) \cdot \lambda_{n-i+1}(\Lambda^*) \leq n$
> **Scope**
> - $\Lambda$: a lattice of rank $n$, with dual $\Lambda^*$.
> - $i \in [n]$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $$1 \leq \lambda_i(\Lambda) \cdot \lambda_{n - i + 1}(\Lambda^*),$$
> $$\lambda_i(\Lambda) \cdot \lambda_{n - i + 1}(\Lambda^*) \leq n.$$
>
> `[SSEKYZ22 Lem 5]` `[Reg04 L11 Thm 1, Rem 1]`

## Variant

### Integer Lattices

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $m \geq 1$: the dimension.

> [!definition] Integer Lattices
> An **integral (or integer) lattice** is a lattice all of whose vectors have integer coordinates. Equivalently, an integral lattice is an additive subgroup of $\mathbb Z^m$ for some $m \geq 1$.
>
> `[HPS]`

### $q$-ary Lattices

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $q$: a positive integer, the modulus.
> - $n \leq l$: positive integers.
> - $A \in \mathbb Z_q^{l \times n}$: a matrix.

> [!definition] $q$-ary Lattices
> The two $l$-dimensional **$q$-ary lattices** of $A$ are
> $$\Lambda_q^\perp (A) = \{x \in \mathbb Z^l \;|\; x^T A = 0 \bmod q\},$$
> $$\Lambda_q(A) = \{v \in \mathbb Z^l \;|\; v = As \bmod q \text{ for some } s \in \mathbb Z^n\}.$$
>
> `[SSEKYZ22 Def 1]`

> [!remark] Notation in the literature
> [GPV08 §5.1] and [MP12 §2.2] take $A \in \mathbb Z_q^{n \times m}$, the transpose of the matrix above, and write
> - $\Lambda^\perp(A) = \{\mathbf e \in \mathbb Z^m : A\mathbf e = 0 \bmod q\}$,
> - $\Lambda(A^T) = \{\mathbf y \in \mathbb Z^m : \mathbf y = A^T \mathbf s \bmod q \text{ for some } \mathbf s \in \mathbb Z^n\}$.
