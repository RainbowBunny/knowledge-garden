## Basic Definition

> [!definition] Lattices
> Let $\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n \in \mathbb R^m$ be a set of linearly independent vectors. The **lattice** $\Lambda$ generated by $\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n$ is the set of linear combinations of $\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_n$ with coefficients in $\mathbb Z$:
> $$\Lambda = \{a_1 \textbf{v}_1 + a_2 \textbf{v}_2 + \cdots + a_n \textbf{v}_n : a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \in \mathbb Z\}.$$
> Another formulation is let $V = [\mathbf{v}_1, \cdots, \mathbf{v}_n]$, then $\Lambda = \{Vx: x \in \mathbb Z^m\}$

> [!proposition]
> Any two bases for a lattice $L$ are related by a matrix having integer coefficients and determinant equal to $\pm 1$.

> [!theorem]
> A subset of $\mathbb R^m$ is a lattice if and only if it is a [[knowledge/math/algebra/Algebra Structure#Group\|discrete additive subgroup]].

### Integer Lattices

> [!definition] Integer Lattices
> An **integral (or integer) lattice** is a lattice all of whose vectors have integer coordinates. Equivalently, an integral lattice is an additive subgroup of $\mathbb Z^m$ for some $m \geq 1$.

### $q$-ary Lattices

> [!definition] $q$-ary Lattices
> For any positive integer $n \leq l$ and $q$, and matrix $A \in \mathbb Z_q^{l \times n}$ define the following $l$-dimensional lattices:
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \Lambda_q^\perp (A) &= \{x \in \mathbb Z^l \;|\; x^T A = 0 \bmod q\} \\
> \Lambda_q(A) &= \{v \in \mathbb Z^l \;|\; v = As \bmod q \text{ for some } s \in \mathbb Z^n\}
> \end{align}$$

### Fundamental Domain

> [!definition] Fundamental Domain
> Let $L$ be a lattice of dimension $n$ and let $\textbf{v}_1, \textbf{v}_2, \dots, \textbf{v}_n$ be a basis for $L$. The **fundamental domain (or fundamental parallelepiped)** for $L$ corresponding to this basis is the set $$\mathcal F(\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n) = \{t_1 \textbf{v}_1 + t_2 \textbf{v}_2 + \cdots + t_n \textbf{v}_n : 0 \leq t_i < 1\}.$$

> [!proposition] 
> Let $L \subset \mathbb R^n$ be a lattice of dimension $n$ and let $\mathcal F$ be a fundamental domain for $L$. Then every vector $\textbf{w} \in \mathbb R^n$ can be written in the form $$\textbf{w} = \textbf{t} + \textbf{v} \text{ for a unique } \textbf{t} \in \mathcal F \text{ and a unique } \textbf{v} \in L.$$ Equivalently, the union of the translated fundamental domains $$\mathcal F + \textbf{v} = \{\textbf{t} + \textbf{v} : \textbf{t} \in \mathcal F\}$$ as $\textbf{v}$ ranges over the vectors in the lattice $L$ exactly covers $\mathbb R^n$.

### Volume

> [!definition] Covolume
> Let $L$ be a lattice of dimension $n$ and let $\mathcal F$ be a fundamental domain for $L$. Then the $n$-dimensional volume of $\mathcal F$ is called the **determinant** of $L$ (or sometimes the **covolume** of $L$). It is denoted by $\det(L)$.

> [!proposition] Hadamard's Inequality
> Let $L$ be a lattice, take any basis $\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n$ for $L$, and let $\mathcal F$ be a fundamental function for $L$. Then $$\det L = \text{Vol}(\mathcal F) \leq ||\textbf{v}_1|| \; ||\textbf{v}_2|| \cdots ||\textbf{v}_n||.$$

> [!proposition]
> Let $L \subset \mathbb R^n$ be a lattice of dimension $n$, let $\textbf{v}_1, \textbf{v}_2, \dots, \textbf{v}_n$ be a basis for $L$, and let $\mathcal F = \mathcal F(\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n)$ be the associated fundamental domain. Write the coordinates of the $i^{\text{th}}$ basis vector as $$\textbf{v}_i = (r_{i1}, r_{i2}, \dots, r_{in})$$ and use the coordinates of the $\textbf{v}_i$ as the rows of a matrix, $$F = F(\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n) = \begin{bmatrix}
> r_{11} & r_{12} & \cdots & r_{1n} \\
> r_{21} & r_{22} & \cdots & r_{2n} \\
> \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
> r_{n1} & r_{n2} & \cdots & r_{nn}
> \end{bmatrix}.$$
> Then the volume of $\mathcal F$ is given by the formula $$\text{Vol}(\mathcal F(\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n)) = |\det(F(\textbf{v}_1, \dots, \textbf{v}_n))|.$$

> [!corollary]
> Let $L \subset \mathbb R^n$ be a lattice of dimension $n$. Then every fundamental domain for $L$ has the same volume. Hence $\det(L)$ is an invariant of the lattice $L$, independent of the particular fundamental domain used to compute it.

## Smoothing Parameter

> [!definition] Smoothing Parameter
> For an $n$-dimensional lattice $\Lambda \subseteq \mathbb Z^n$ and a positive real $\epsilon > 0$, the smoothing parameter $\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ is the smallest real $r > 0$, such that $\rho_{1 / r}(\Lambda^* \backslash \{0\}) \leq \epsilon$ with $\rho$ is the [[knowledge/math/algebra/structures/set theory/functions/Unnormalized Gaussian Function#Basic Definition\|Unnormalized Gaussian Function]].

> [!lemma] Consequence of Smoothing
> For any $\epsilon \in (0, 1), r \geq \eta_\epsilon(\Lambda)$ (smoothing parameter) and $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb R^n$, we have
> $$\rho_r(\mathbf{c} + \Lambda) \in \left [ (1 + \epsilon) / (1 - \epsilon), 1 \right ] \cdot \rho_r(\Lambda)$$

> [!lemma] Upper-Bound of Smoothing Parameter
> For real $\epsilon > 0$, we have
> $$\eta_\epsilon(\Lambda) \leq \frac{\sqrt{\ln(2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1})) / \pi}}{\lambda_1^{\infty}(\Lambda^*)}.$$ 

> [!lemma] Sandwiches of Smoothing Parameter
> For $\epsilon \in (0, 1 / 2)$, we have
> $$\lambda_n(\Lambda) / \sqrt{n} \leq \eta_\epsilon(\Lambda) \leq \lambda_n(\Lambda) \cdot \sqrt{\ln (2n(1 + \epsilon^{-1})) / \pi}.$$

## Transference

> [!lemma]
> For all $i \in [n]$, we have
> $$1 \leq \lambda_i(\Lambda) \cdot \lambda_{n - i + 1}(\Lambda^*) \leq n$$
