## Intuition

> [!intuition] Why
> Listing every open set is impractical. Most topologies are described by a few simple open sets, such as intervals or balls, together with everything built from them; a basis makes that precise, and the lemmas say when two descriptions give the same topology.

## Definition

> [!definition] Basis for a Topology
> A **basis** for a topology on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal B$ of subsets of $X$, the **basis elements**, such that
> - every $x \in X$ lies in at least one $B \in \mathcal B$;
> - if $x \in B_1 \cap B_2$ with $B_1, B_2 \in \mathcal B$, there is $B_3 \in \mathcal B$ with $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2$.
>
> `[Mun00 §13]`, `[Hat Prop 1.2]`. `[Mor Prop 2.2.8]` phrases the second condition as: $B_1 \cap B_2$ is a union of basis elements.

> [!definition] Topology Generated by a Basis
> The **topology generated by** $\mathcal B$ is
> $$\mathcal T = \{U \subseteq X : \forall x \in U\; \exists B \in \mathcal B: x \in B \subseteq U\}.$$
> `[Mun00 §13]`

### Subbasis

> [!definition] Subbasis
> A **subbasis** for a topology on $X$ is a collection $\mathcal S$ of subsets of $X$ whose union is $X$. The **topology generated by** $\mathcal S$ is the collection of all unions of finite intersections of elements of $\mathcal S$.
>
> `[Mun00 §13]`, `[Møl Def 2.6]`: both require $\mathcal S$ to cover $X$.

## Property

> [!lemma] Unions of basis elements — the generated topology, described from below
> **Scope**
> - $\mathcal B$ a basis on $X$, generating $\mathcal T$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $\mathcal T$ is a topology on $X$.
> $\mathcal T$ is the collection of all unions of elements of $\mathcal B$.
>
> `[Mun00 §13, Lemma 13.1]`. `[Hat p. 7]` and `[Mor Def 2.2.2]` take the second line as the definition of a basis of a given topology.

> [!proof] Why
> - $\emptyset \in \mathcal T$ vacuously, and $X \in \mathcal T$ by the first condition. Unions of members of $\mathcal T$ are in $\mathcal T$ directly. For $U_1, U_2 \in \mathcal T$ and $x \in U_1 \cap U_2$, choose $x \in B_i \subseteq U_i$; the second condition gives $x \in B_3 \subseteq B_1 \cap B_2 \subseteq U_1 \cap U_2$. Finite intersections follow by induction.
> - Each $B \in \mathcal B$ lies in $\mathcal T$, hence so does every union of basis elements. Conversely, every $U \in \mathcal T$ equals $\bigcup \{B \in \mathcal B : B \subseteq U\}$.

> [!lemma] Recognizing a basis — enough small open sets
> **Scope**
> - $(X, \mathcal T)$ a [[knowledge/math/topology/Topological Space\|Topological Space]]; $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathcal T$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> If for every open $U$ and every $x \in U$ some $C \in \mathcal C$ satisfies $x \in C \subseteq U$, then $\mathcal C$ is a basis, and it generates $\mathcal T$.
>
> `[Mun00 Lemma 13.2]`. `[Møl Prop 2.8]`: a basis generates $\mathcal T$ iff its elements are open and every open set is a union of them.

> [!lemma] Comparing topologies through bases — local refinement
> **Scope**
> - $\mathcal B$, $\mathcal B'$ bases on $X$, generating $\mathcal T$, $\mathcal T'$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> $\mathcal T'$ is [[knowledge/math/topology/Topological Space#Finer and coarser\|finer]] than $\mathcal T$ $\iff$ for each $x \in X$ and each $B \in \mathcal B$ containing $x$, some $B' \in \mathcal B'$ satisfies $x \in B' \subseteq B$.
>
> `[Mun00 Lemma 13.3]`. `[Møl Lemma 2.10]` puts it as: $\mathcal T \subseteq \mathcal T'$ iff every element of $\mathcal B$ is open in $\mathcal T'$.

> [!proposition] Subbases — finite intersections form a basis
> **Scope**
> - $\mathcal S$ a subbasis on $X$.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> The finite intersections of elements of $\mathcal S$ form a basis, so the topology generated by $\mathcal S$ is a topology.
> It is the coarsest topology on $X$ containing $\mathcal S$.
>
> `[Mun00 §13]` for the first line. The second holds because a topology containing $\mathcal S$ contains its finite intersections and their unions; `[Møl Def 2.6]` describes generated topologies this way.

## Example

> [!example] Bases of familiar topologies
> - The open intervals $(a, b)$ are a basis for the standard topology on $\mathbb R$; the open rays $(a, \infty)$, $(-\infty, b)$ are a subbasis but not a basis `[Møl Ex 2.9(1)]`: $(0, 1)$ is an intersection of two rays and contains no ray. See [[knowledge/math/topology/example/Topologies on the Real Line\|Topologies on the Real Line]].
> - The open rectangles $(a, b) \times (c, d)$ are a basis for the usual topology of $\mathbb R^2$ `[Mor Ex 2.2.9]`; so are the open discs `[Mun00 §13]`.
> - The open balls of a metric space ([[knowledge/math/topology/Topological Space#Metric topology\|Topological Space#Metric topology]]).
> - The singletons $\{x\}$ are a basis for the discrete topology.

> [!example] A collection that is not a basis
> On $X = \{1, 2, 3\}$, the collection $\{\{1, 2\}, \{2, 3\}\}$ covers $X$, but $2 \in \{1, 2\} \cap \{2, 3\} = \{2\}$ and no member lies inside $\{2\}$. As a subbasis it generates $\{\emptyset, \{2\}, \{1, 2\}, \{2, 3\}, X\}$.
