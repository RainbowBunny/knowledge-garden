## Intuition

> [!intuition] Why
> An ordered set already knows what lying between two points means. The order topology turns that into nearness, so the usual topology of ℝ, the topology of the ordinals and the ordered square all come from their orders.

## Definition

> [!definition] Parameters
> - $(X, <)$: a [[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Strict Total Order\|simply ordered set]] with more than one element; intervals and rays as in [[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Strict Total Order#Intervals and rays\|Strict Total Order#Intervals and rays]].

> [!definition] Order Topology
> Requires:: [[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Strict Total Order\|Strict Total Order]], [[knowledge/math/topology/Basis for a Topology\|Basis for a Topology]]
>
> ---
> The **order topology** on $X$ is the topology generated by the basis consisting of
> - all open intervals $(a, b)$ in $X$;
> - all intervals $[a_0, b)$, where $a_0$ is the smallest element of $X$, if there is one;
> - all intervals $(a, b_0]$, where $b_0$ is the largest element of $X$, if there is one.
>
> `[Mun00 §14]`

> [!remark] Rays generate the same topology
> The open rays $(a, +\infty)$ and $(-\infty, a)$ are open in the order topology and form a [[knowledge/math/topology/Basis for a Topology#Subbasis\|subbasis]] for it `[Mun00 §14]`: $(a, b) = (a, +\infty) \cap (-\infty, b)$, $[a_0, b) = (-\infty, b)$ and $(a, b_0] = (a, +\infty)$. `[Møl Def 2.13]` takes this as the definition, and recovers the basis of intervals and rays in `[Møl Lemma 2.14]`.

## Property

### Subspaces and convex sets

> [!theorem] Convex subsets — the order topology is the subspace topology
> **Scope**
> - $X$ with the order topology; $Y \subseteq X$ with the restricted order ([[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Strict Total Order#Property\|Strict Total Order#Property]]), assumed to have more than one element.
>
> ---
> **Property**
> The order topology on $Y$ is coarser than the [[knowledge/math/topology/Subspace Topology\|subspace topology]] on $Y$ `[Møl Lemma 2.27]`.
> If $Y$ is [[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Strict Total Order#Convex subsets\|convex]] in $X$, the two topologies on $Y$ are equal `[Mun00 Thm 16.4]` `[Møl Lemma 2.27]`.

> [!proof] Why
> For $a \in Y$, the ray $\{y \in Y : a < y\}$ of $Y$ equals $Y \cap (a, +\infty)$, so every subbasis element of the order topology on $Y$ is open in the subspace topology. Conversely, the sets $Y \cap (a, +\infty)$ and $Y \cap (-\infty, a)$ with $a \in X$ form a subbasis of the subspace topology `[Møl Lemma 2.23]`. If $a \in Y$ they are rays of $Y$. If $a \notin Y$ and $Y$ is convex, then $a$ lies below all of $Y$ or above all of $Y$, so each of them is $Y$ or $\emptyset$. `[Mun00 Thm 16.4]`

## Example

> [!example] Order topologies
> - $\mathbb R$: the standard topology `[Mun00 §14]` `[Møl Ex 2.15(1)]` ([[knowledge/math/topology/example/Topologies on the Real Line\|Topologies on the Real Line]]).
> - $\mathbb Z_{>0}$: the discrete topology, since $\{1\} = [1, 2)$ and $\{n\} = (n - 1, n + 1)$ for $n > 1$ `[Mun00 §14]` `[Møl Ex 2.15(3)]`.
> - $\mathbb R \times \mathbb R$ in the [[knowledge/math/set theory/order/Dictionary Order\|Dictionary Order]]: the intervals $(a \times b, a \times d)$ with $b < d$ form a basis `[Mun00 §14]`, so each vertical line $\{a\} \times \mathbb R$ is open.
> - $\{1, 2\} \times \mathbb Z_{>0}$ in the dictionary order is not discrete: every basis element containing $(2, 1)$ contains elements $(1, n)$ `[Mun00 §14]`.

> [!example] Where subspace and order topology part ways
> - $Y = [0, 1] \subseteq \mathbb R$ is convex, so the two topologies agree.
> - $Y = [0, 1) \cup \{2\} \subseteq \mathbb R$ is not convex. $\{2\} = Y \cap (3/2, 5/2)$ is open in the subspace topology. In the order topology of $Y$, every basis element containing $2$ has the form $\{y \in Y : a < y \leq 2\}$ with $a \in Y$, and contains points below $2$; with the order topology, $Y$ looks like $[0, 1]$, to which it is order-isomorphic `[Mun00 §16]`.
> - The **ordered square** $I_o^2$ is $I \times I$, $I = [0, 1]$, in the dictionary order topology. $\{1/2\} \times (1/2, 1]$ is open in the subspace topology from $\mathbb R \times \mathbb R$ in the dictionary order, but not in $I_o^2$: every interval of $I \times I$ containing $1/2 \times 1$ contains points $x \times 0$ with $x > 1/2$ `[Mun00 §16 Ex 3]` `[Møl Ex 2.15(7), 2.28(3)]`.
> - $\mathbb Z_{>0} \times \mathbb Z_{>0}$ is not convex in $\mathbb Z \times \mathbb Z$ (dictionary orders); its subspace topology is discrete and its order topology is not `[Møl Ex 2.28(2)]`.
> - Convexity is sufficient, not necessary: $\mathbb Q \subseteq \mathbb R$ is not convex, yet the two topologies on $\mathbb Q$ agree `[Møl Ex 2.28(5)]`.
